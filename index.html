Campaign Overview

May 1 – September 14, 2026 · End of Campaign

📋 Performance Summary

Meta · May 4 – Sep 7 · Closed TikTok · May 11 – Sep 7 · Closed Walmart · Old Model (May) + New Model (June–Sep) · Closed Display · May 20 – Sep 7 · Closed Off Story · May 27 – Sep 7 · Closed

Retail velocity has cooled from its June peak, but remains well above January levels. Weekly velocity peaked at $47.45/store in the 4 weeks ending Jun 14, then eased to $35.73/store as of Aug 19 — still 137% above the January baseline, but down 25% from the peak. Distribution (ACV) has ticked down slightly too (28.2% → 26.2%), and 4-week dollar sales have fallen from $3.06M to $1.82M over the same stretch. Experian Mosaic segmentation still shows "Families in Motion" as the clear top-buying household segment — its index actually strengthened further, from 189 to 222, in the latest refresh. See the Market Performance tab for the full trend and retailer breakdown.

Meta closed as the campaign's standout for efficiency. The Engagement campaign finished at 54,491 post engagements at a $0.12 CPE and an 18.50% engagement rate that held steady across the full 18-week flight. The Traffic/Retargeting campaign closed at 20,108 landing page views at just $0.17 per visit, with Frequency finishing at 1.96–2.65 across the two campaigns — comfortably in the healthy 1.5–3.0× range wire-to-wire. Both campaigns wrapped delivery on September 7.

TikTok closed at 5.44M impressions and a $1.87 CPM final — still less than half the $3–4+ benchmark across the full flight. The deceleration flagged the last two updates held through close: the final stretch delivered consistently below the campaign's early pace, though CPM efficiency never slipped out of strong territory.

Display closed at 30.65M Custom/Carousel impressions across 11 retail partners (corrected from 16 shown previously — see note below) and $183,871 in spend, with Kroger, Smart & Final, and Walmart leading the final leaderboard. The Off Story campaign closed at 2,043,116 impressions across the same 11 retail partners at a strong 1.52% CTR; story completion rate finished at 62.8%, continuing its decline from 63.0% last update. Third-party retail sales data (ABCS) attributes $588,776 in sales to these Display campaigns May 20–Jul 31 at a 4.30× blended ROAS — the last fully reconciled read. Preliminary monthly totals now extend through August on the Display tab; the fully reconciled ABCS file is expected Oct 31, 2026, so these figures may still move.

Walmart closed as the final platform in the flight. New Model campaigns (June 1–Sep 14) finished at $30,845 in attributed sales on $34,255 in spend for a 0.90× blended ROAS — a modest step up from the 0.88× mid-flight read. Remarketing – Non-Buyers closed as the strongest ad group at 1.39× ROAS, followed by Contextual Targeting at 0.91×; Behavioral, Keywords, and Lapsed-Buyer targeting all finished under 1.0×. Old Model campaigns (May 12–31) remain closed at 2.40× blended ROAS on $20,017 in attributed sales, unchanged.

Off Story Highlights

Key Takeaways

Creative Performance

Meta + TikTok · Final — May 1 – Sep 7, 2026
📊 End-of-Campaign Creative Analysis Meta and TikTok delivery both closed Sep 7 — final creative-level results below, rebuilt for the full flight rather than a single week. These are the learnings to carry into the next creative brief: what earned its spot, what didn't, and what to try next.

Meta Ads — Engagement Campaign

Final · May 1 – Sep 7
✅ What Worked
  • Static V1 was the most efficient broad-audience creative all flight: a 20.7% engagement rate at $0.12 CPE, both better than Static V2 (15.4% rate, $0.13 CPE) even though V2 carried more of the budget.
  • The World Cup host-city audience outperformed the broad-retailer audience on engagement rate throughout — 21–23% vs. 15–21% broad — regardless of which creative was running. That's the standout learning: the geo-targeted audience itself was the driver, not just the World Cup theme.
  • The mid-flight creative swap was seamless. When the soccer-themed creative was replaced with amusement-park creative in the same host-city audience after the World Cup ended, performance didn't dip — Soccer-phase engagement rate was 21.2% vs. 23.1% for the Amusement Park phase that followed. Reusing a proven, already-warmed-up audience with refreshed seasonal creative is a repeatable playbook.
📎 For the Next Brief
  • Static V2 drove the most raw volume (22,234 engagements) but at a real efficiency cost vs. V1.
  • No underperforming Engagement creative to flag this campaign — both concepts delivered efficiently; the difference is by degree, not a pause call.

TikTok — Creative Analysis

Reach Campaign · Final · May 11 – Sep 7
Each concept ran in two parallel targeting splits (TikTok caps how many zip codes one ad group can target); figures below combine both. Not every concept ran the full flight: Carousel OOH and Product Carousel were both paused July 6 for low performance, while Twisting, Magic Hands, and Boxes launched July 27 as a mid-flight creative refresh — tagged in the table below.
✅ What Worked
  • Boxes - Native Concept — launched July 27 as part of the mid-flight refresh — was the standout: the best CTR of any creative (0.19%) and by far the cheapest cost-per-click ($0.96), at a mid-pack $1.85 CPM. The clearest template for the next brief.
  • Native Concept 5 and Native Concept 1 back it up — Concept 5 had the second-best CTR (0.17%), Concept 1 the single cheapest CPM ($1.64) of any creative in market.
⏸️ For the Next Brief
  • Native Concept 4 is the one real flag: it absorbed 45% of the entire TikTok budget ($4,636 of $10,198) yet finished with the worst CPM ($1.96) and one of the weakest CTRs (0.12%) in the mix. It started strong in the May–June read but efficiency eroded as it kept scaling.
  • Native Concepts 2 and 3 were unremarkable in either direction (CTR ~0.12%, CPM ~$1.73–1.83) — fine as supporting rotation, not concepts to lead with.

Case Study — Ibotta Cashback Program
Completed · Jun 10 – Jul 30, 2026

A $1.00-off, item-level Ibotta offer ran Jun 10 – Jul 30, 2026 (51 days live, now expired) at a total spend of $88,515.20. It moved 44,480 units at a 50.93% redemption rate — 4× the 12.67% Frozen Novelties category benchmark. Walmart drove 73.2% of redemptions.

The standout number: in a 3-month lookback, 73% of redeemers had only purchased competitor brands in the frozen category beforehand — not existing Dippin' Dots buyers. Only 1% were already loyal to the brand. This is one of the clearest pieces of evidence anywhere in this data that media/promotion spend is winning trial from competitive buyers, not just rewarding people who'd buy anyway.

End of Campaign

Learnings & Recommendations · May 1 – Sep 7, 2026
✅

What Worked

Wins to carry forward
  • Meta Engagement held an 18.50% engagement rate at just $0.12 CPE across the full 18-week flight — the campaign's clearest efficiency standout, wire-to-wire.
  • TikTok closed at a $1.87 CPM, well under the $3–4 benchmark for the entire flight — efficiency never slipped.
  • The World Cup geo-targeted "Host Cities" Meta audience outperformed the broad-retailer audience on engagement throughout, regardless of which creative was live; the mid-flight swap from soccer to amusement-park imagery once the tournament ended caused no measurable dip.
  • TikTok's "Boxes – Native Concept" — launched July 27 as part of a mid-flight creative refresh — delivered the best CTR/CPC of any TikTok creative, becoming the clearest template for the next brief.
  • Underperforming creative was cut proactively, not left to run — OOH Carousel and Carousel V1 (Meta Traffic/Retargeting) and Carousel OOH and Product Carousel (TikTok) were all paused July 6 for weak results, with fresh concepts (Product Carousel V2, and TikTok's Twisting, Magic Hands, and Boxes) launching July 27 to keep the back half of the flight strong.
  • Old Model Walmart (May) closed at a strong 2.40× ROAS, led by Contextual targeting at 3.74×.
  • "Families in Motion" stayed the top-indexing Mosaic segment and actually strengthened over the flight (189 → 222 index), validating the audience targeting strategy.
  • Product Carousel was the standout format on Meta Traffic/Retargeting — the most efficient creative in both audiences ($0.166/LPV broad-retailer, $0.155/LPV World Cup host-city).
🔄

What We'd Do Differently

Room to improve
  • Walmart's New Model campaigns closed under 1.0× ROAS (0.90× on $34,255 in spend) — a real disappointment for the flight. Contextual again outperformed Behavioral, Keywords, and Lapsed-Buyer targeting; next time, we'd concentrate Walmart Connect budget on Contextual alone and shift the rest to Display, which already covers Walmart's retailers.
  • TikTok's "Native Concept 4" absorbed 45% of total TikTok spend but finished with the worst CPM of any concept — a creative-fatigue-at-scale pattern that should be capped earlier next time rather than let one concept dominate delivery.
  • Off Story's completion-rate decline was concentrated in the same handful of retailers all flight (Schnucks, Wegmans, Weis). Retailer-level sales don't point to an early-click-through explanation — Wegmans, the one of the three we can isolate, accounted for only 0.3% of Display-attributed sales through July — so worth testing a shorter story format or a stronger mid-story hook for the retailers that consistently lagged.
🎯

Recommendations

For the next campaign
  • Narrow Walmart Connect budget to Contextual targeting only, and shift the rest to Display — Contextual was the one Walmart Connect tactic that worked this flight, and Display already reaches Walmart's retailers.
  • Keep building event-tied geo-targeting into the initial media plan the way this flight's World Cup host-city audience was — it validated the approach again: the audience itself drove the lift, more than the specific seasonal creative theme. Look for a similar cultural or event tie-in to plan around next time.
  • Set a spend cap or rotation trigger per TikTok creative concept so no single concept can absorb 45%+ of budget while losing efficiency.
  • Consider allocating retargeting creative by audience: Product Carousel content leans hardest with 18–24 Female, while OOH/lifestyle imagery resonates more with 65+ Female — a one-size format mix left performance on the table with both groups.
  • Build a mid-flight creative refresh into the plan as a scheduled checkpoint rather than a reactive fix — this flight's July 27 refresh (Product Carousel V2, Twisting, Magic Hands, Boxes) is exactly how Boxes became TikTok's strongest concept.
